1 · The questions it answers

If the transition is slower or faster, what happens to demand?

Light duty vehicles

How fast do battery electric and plug-in hybrid cars take over new sales in each region, and how many combustion-engine cars are still sold each year?

Heavy duty vehicles

How fast do electric trucks and buses take over new sales in China, the USA and the rest of the world, and what is left for combustion engines?

Oil outside road transport

How does oil use in petrochemicals, shipping, aviation and buildings/power change as activity grows, efficiency improves, and other fuels take a share?

Each answer comes in three scenario cases: Slower Transition, Base Case and Faster Transition. The gap between them shows how much the outcome depends on the speed of change.

3 · How it works

Assumptions in, forecasts out

Actuals

The last measured year (2024) from the source data: vehicles sold, oil used, activity.

→

Assumptions

A few percentages per case for 2025, 2030, 2035, 2040 and 2050: growth rates, efficiency gains, shares of electric or other fuels.

→

Forecast

Every year from 2025 to 2050 is calculated from the 2024 actual and those assumptions.

Vehicles

Grow the total market, take the electric share of it, and what remains is combustion engines (IC Only).

Shipping and aviation

Activity × oil per unit of activity, minus the share served by other fuels. Petrochemicals and buildings/power simply grow at a rate.

The full method, with formulas and a worked example, is in the app's first page and in docs/methodology/.

4 · What you can do with it

Change a number, see the forecast move

  • Explore: edit any assumption and see the new forecast drawn as a dashed line next to the saved one, before you save anything.
  • Compare: show all three scenario cases side by side, by region, powertrain or sector.
  • Save: keep a set of assumptions as a named scenario and make it the one everyone sees.
  • Take the numbers away: every chart has a table view that can be sorted and downloaded.

Who it is for

People who want to test transition assumptions and see their effect on demand quickly, without building a model each time.

Everyone signs in with one shared login, so saved scenarios are shared by the whole team.

5 · What it is not

Read the results with these limits in mind

Projections, not predictions

Every number follows from the assumptions. There is no statistical fitting and no price or economic modelling.

Three separate models

Vehicle sales do not feed oil consumption, and there is no total oil demand figure.

One line per case

No uncertainty bands: the spread between Slower and Faster Transition is the only range shown.

"Sales" are production

GlobalData records vehicles where they are built, so a region's "sales" can differ from what is sold there.

Fleet is not the vehicle stock

It adds up sales since 2020 and never retires a vehicle; older vehicles are left out.

No fuel use for vehicles

The vehicle models count vehicles, not kilometres driven or fuel burned.
